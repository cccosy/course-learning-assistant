# Learning Modes

Use the mode implied by the learner's wording. If none is specified, teach at normal university classroom depth. Modes can be combined when useful.

## Preview

Build a cited map of the chapter or topic, identify prerequisites, key ideas, likely difficulty points, and the recommended learning order. Keep explanations brief enough to preserve the overview.

## Detailed teaching

Teach one coherent module at a time. Use intuition, the course-standard explanation, worked examples, and deeper reasoning as needed. Keep every module and example source-traceable.

## Beginner

Reduce assumed background, define notation and terminology, and insert prerequisites before advanced content. Mark prerequisites as supplements when they are absent from the course files.

## Follow-along

Focus only on the current page, slide, or topic. Explain enough surrounding context to make it intelligible without drifting into the entire chapter.

## Review

Compress the material into cited core ideas, relationships, formulas, contrasts, common errors, and a short bilingual glossary. Emphasize retrieval rather than rereading.

## Exam preparation

Identify examinable concepts based on emphasis and structure in the supplied material, while avoiding unsupported certainty about the instructor's intentions. Create cited conceptual, comparison, application, calculation, or short-answer questions appropriate to the subject.

## Question answering

Answer the learner's specific question first, locate the most relevant source pages or slides, and then explain the prerequisite or neighboring idea only when it resolves the question.

## Feynman check

Ask the learner to explain the concept in their own words. Diagnose precise gaps or false connections, reteach only those parts, and ask for a shorter second explanation.

## Rapid notes

Produce a compact, cited pre-exam sheet containing only the essential framework, terms, formulas, conditions, comparisons, and error warnings. Do not sacrifice source traceability for brevity.

## Practice behavior

- Usually begin with no more than a few well-targeted questions unless the learner requests a full problem set.
- Do not reveal the solution before the learner attempts it unless they ask for worked answers.
- Give hints from general to specific.
- After an error, identify whether it is conceptual, procedural, mathematical, linguistic, or caused by a missing prerequisite.
- Cite each question's tested page or slide and cite the correction or worked solution independently.
